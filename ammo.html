Ammo line

3 deployers · 7 × 2 · 12 SU/RPM

A 5-block belt with three deployers. Feed casings into the input chest and finished rounds land in the output chest. All 12 standard calibers use the same two steps (gunpowder pinch, then lead nugget), so you switch caliber just by feeding a different casing. You can mix calibers in one chest.

For heavy rounds, swap D1 and D2 for one batch, then swap back. The standard calibers are 9mm, .45 ACP, .357, .50 AE, 12g, 5.56, 5.7, 7.62×39, .30-06, .308, 7.62×54 and .338. Standard casings come 4 per brass ingot from the IE Metal Press.

Build steps

  1. Lay a 5-long belt from x1 to x5. Put the input chest at x0, then place an andesite funnel on its face so it sits on the belt. Placed normally, a funnel pulls from the chest.
  2. Put the output chest at x6 and sneak-place its funnel so it inserts. A wrench flips a funnel between pulling and inserting.
  3. Put deployers D1–D3 on y2 above x2–x4, facing down. Their shaft ends must point east–west so they pass rotation along the row. Wrench them if they don't.
  4. Build the power tower behind x1: gearbox (y0), vertical shaft (y1), gearbox (y2), then a gearbox in front of D1. Feed power in from above.
  5. Check the belt runs from x1 toward x5. If it runs backwards, put one more gearbox in the incoming shaft (straight through a gearbox reverses rotation).
  6. Add a hopper on each deployer, and a chest on top of each hopper if you want long runs between refills.

Speed: a deployer step takes about 2 s at 32 RPM and 1 s at 64 RPM, so expect roughly one round every 1–2 s.

Parts ring

6 deployers · 9 × 5 · 24 SU/RPM

Gun-part kits loop 4 times through six steps, so a straight line would need 24 deployers. Here four belts form a loop: the item keeps circling past six stations until it's finished, then a filtered brass funnel takes it off. All 7 gun-part kits and the attachment parts run on this one ring. You only swap the blueprint and the special material.

S2–S5 hold the same items for every kit. To change kit, swap the blueprint in S1 and, if needed, the special material in S6. For attachments, S4 keeps its springs and is just skipped.

Build steps

  1. Lay four belts as on layer y0. A runs x1–x8 on the north row, D is x0 (2 long), C runs x0–x7 on the south row, and B is x8 (2 long). Each belt's last tile sits beside the first tile of the next belt.
  2. Right-click belt C at x1 with a shaft to add a pulley there. Fill the middle row: gearbox at x1, then shafts from x2 to x7 into B's pulley.
  3. Power goes in only through the tower at x1 (gearbox y2 → vertical shaft → gearbox y0). Don't drive any belt directly. The four belts only form a loop when that bottom gearbox is fed from above.
  4. Check the flow before loading anything. Belt A must move west, toward D. If it moves east, add one gearbox to the incoming shaft. All four belts reverse together.
  5. Put the deployers on y2 over A: S1 at x7 through S6 at x2. The gearbox at x1 on y2 passes power along the row.
  6. Input: chest north of x8 with an andesite funnel placed normally. Exit: chest south of C at x4 with a sneak-placed brass funnel. Put a Filter on it listing the 8 kits.
  7. Load at most 8 cardboard boxes at a time. A packed loop can jam.

Optional: a mechanical press on y2 above belt C at x1, with its shaft north–south, connects straight to the tower's top gearbox. Then spring wire dropped into the input chest gets pressed once per lap and comes out the exit as a Recoil Spring after 3 laps (add Recoil Spring to the filter). That replaces the Metalworks spring presses, but the springs share the ring with the kits.

Metalworks

saw + 3 presses · 10 × 2 · 28 SU/RPM

This feeds the ring. Steel ingots go in and come out as Steel Plates, because only the first press has a recipe for them. Steel plates go in and the saw cuts each into 3 Spring Wire, then the three presses turn every wire into a Recoil Spring.

Steel Ingot inSteel Plateiron, gold, copper, brass work too
Steel Plate in3 Recoil Springssaw filter: Spring Wire
Per kit13⅓ plates4 to the ring, 8 to components, 1⅓ to springs
  1. A 2-block belt (x1–x2) carries items from the input chest into the saw at x3. Place the saw at belt level facing up, with its shaft north–south, and put a Filter with Spring Wire on it. Steel plate has two cutting recipes, so without a filter the saw alternates between spring wire and steel wire.
  2. Three cogwheels behind x2, x3 and x4 mesh in a row. The saw then spins opposite to both belts, which is what makes all three move items east. The main belt drives the whole chain.
  3. Put the presses on y2 over x5–x7 with their shafts along the row. They're powered from the tower at x8.
  4. If items come back out of the saw toward the input, the line runs backwards. Reverse the incoming shaft.

Rolling mill (rods)

The Crafts & Additions Rolling Mill turns 1 steel ingot into 2 Steel Rods, twice what the pack's wirecutter recipe gives. Stack it: chest, hopper pointing down, mill, hopper, chest, then run a shaft into its side. It takes ingots from the hopper above and lets the hopper below pull rods out. It costs 4 SU/RPM.

Steel Mechanical Components

These are made at the IE Engineer's Workbench with the components blueprint: 2 steel plates + 1 copper ingot each. Craft them by hand, or use an IE Automated Workbench if you have IE power. Each kit needs 4.

Powering these builds

The ammo line needs 12 SU per RPM, the parts ring 24, the Metalworks 28 and the rolling mill 4. That's about 2,200 SU at 32 RPM, which one 64-sail windmill covers. See Rotational power. The IE Metal Press that makes casings runs on electricity: see Electricity.
